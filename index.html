See, Hear, and Understand: Benchmarking Audiovisual Human Speech Understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models

Le Thien Phuc Nguyen*, Zhuoran Yu*, Samuel Low Yu Hang, Subin An, Jeongkik Lee, Yohan Ban, SeungEun Chung, Thanh-Huy Nguyen, Juwan Maeng, Soochahn Lee, Yong Jae Lee
( * equal contribution )
GitHub Paper Dataset Leaderboard

What makes it different

Fusion-grounded design with explicit anchors to prevent unimodal shortcuts.

Benchmark focus

  • Clip length: short, real clips (<25s) to match typical frame budgets.
  • Coverage: 11 audio-visual skills including speaker detection/recognition/counting, speech duration/rate/intensity/pitch, activity, attributes, visual counting, and speech recognition.
  • Diversity: 25.8% ≤2 speakers, 24.6% have 3, 18.1% have 4, 31.5% have ≥5.

Anchored questions

Audio tasks use visual anchors; visual tasks use audio anchors; speaker reasoning mixes both to force cross-modal grounding.

  • Find the visual cue, then listen in-window to count mentions.
  • Locate the utterance, then inspect the video for the answer.
  • Track who/when/where with mixed anchors and answer cues.

Dataset at a glance

  • Scale: 3,212 fusion-grounded MCQs from real-world clips.
  • Modality: aligned audio + video; questions require cross-modal reasoning.
  • Source: curated clips with expert annotations for temporal accuracy.
